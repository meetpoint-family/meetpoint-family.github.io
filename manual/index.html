MeetPoint Manual

After an earthquake, a fire or a long blackout, calls fail and families can't reach each other. MeetPoint keeps your family's plan on every phone — where to meet, who does what — and gets your news through by whatever still works: text message, internet, phones nearby or Meshtastic radio.

Video tour, 3½ minutes, narrated. It follows the Walker family: making the plan, emergency points, inviting the family, sending “I'm OK” on the day and how the family confirms it, the family list and map, the four networks (switching one off, and your own relay server if you like) and the emergency number.

01Before you start

Make the plan now, while everything works, and make it together. A plan only helps if everyone knows it — and everyone in the family needs MeetPoint on their own phone, iPhone or Android.

One person makes the family and its plan (chapter 02). Everyone else joins with an invite from that person's phone (chapter 03). From then on, the phones keep each other up to date on their own.

Permissions

On iPhone, choose Allow While Using App first, then Change to Always Allow when iOS asks. On Android, While using the app is enough: MeetPoint stays running with its notification.

Keep MeetPoint running. It works in the background, also with the screen locked. Don't swipe it away from the recent apps: a closed MeetPoint can't pass messages on, and your family sees you as Offline.

What works with no internet: the whole plan, GPS and distances, the saved map, phones nearby, the Meshtastic radio and text messages. Only searching for an address and downloading the map need internet.

02Making the family

On the first screen, tap Make a New Plan. Only one person does this; the others join afterwards.

About you

Type your name and your mobile number, and pick your dot for the map: a colour and one or two letters. The number lets the family text you when there's no internet. Names are unique in the family. Tap ✓.

Meeting points

A new plan starts with the three meeting points that civil protection guides recommend. Everyone goes to point 1. If it can't be reached, or nobody arrives in time, they go to the next one.

PointWhat it is for
1Near homeSomewhere you can walk to in a few minutes, away from buildings: a square, a park, a car park. For a fire or an earthquake at home.
2Out of the neighborhoodSomewhere everyone knows, further away, for when you can't get home or the area is closed.
3Out of townA relative's or friend's house, if the whole town has to leave.

Open ⚙︎ (top right of the plan) and tap a meeting point. Give it a name and a note on where exactly to wait (“by the fountain”), then place it: tap the map, use Use Where I Am Now while you are standing there (the most exact), or search an address (needs internet). ✓ keeps it.

Add Meeting Point adds another one; drag to change the order, swipe left to delete. Move on if nobody arrives with Wait before moving on (30 minutes unless you change it) says how long to wait at a point before going to the next one.

Who does what, and notes

Under Who Does What, tap Add Task, choose who (a person, or Anyone) and write the job: “Picks up Ana at school”, “Takes the emergency bag and the dog”, “Turns off the gas”. Notes is for everything else: medication, documents, where the emergency bag is.

Invite the family

In ⚙︎, tap Invite Family — QR Code or Link. Each person scans the QR code with their phone, or opens the link you send them (Send Invite Link). Every phone in the family then adds them on its own.

Only the person who made the family changes the plan — points, tasks, notes. Their changes reach every phone over the internet or phones nearby. Everyone else can change their own name, number and dot, and add emergency points.

Emergency points

On the day, a place to meet that isn't in the plan — the road is closed, the family is together somewhere else. Anyone can add one under ⚙︎ → Emergency Points → Add Emergency Point. It goes to the whole family and shows in red with a warning sign on the map, until the family's creator, or whoever added it, takes it off.

03Joining a family

Someone in your family already made the plan? You join from your own phone.

  1. Install MeetPoint and open it.
  2. Scan the invite QR code on their phone (with MeetPoint's scanner, or the iPhone's Camera), or open the invite link they sent you.
  3. Type your name and, if you like, your mobile number, and pick your dot. Tap ✓.

The plan opens, and every phone in the family adds you. Only you can change your name, number and dot afterwards, on your own phone.

Open an invite again later, when the plan changed while you were away: MeetPoint offers Update My Plan, which brings in the newer points and people and keeps who you are.

Share the invite only with your family. Whoever has it can join and read the family's messages.

04The plan screen

MeetPoint opens straight on the plan, with or without a network. From top to bottom:

  • The family's name and ⚙︎ — the settings: plan, people, invite, networks, language.
  • Network icons — SMS, Internet, Nearby, Radio, each in the colour of how it's doing right now (chapter 08). Tap them for the details.
  • I'm OK and Help — your news to the family (chapter 05).
  • Send My Situation by SMS — the text message, when you choose to send one (chapter 09). Only on phones that can send texts.
  • Family — you first, then everyone else: how they are, how news reaches them and where they are (chapter 06). Touch and hold a person to call or text them.
  • Map — the meeting points and the family. Tap it for the full map with distances (chapter 07).
  • Who Does What — your own tasks have a star.
  • Emergency — the emergency numbers for where you are, and your position (chapter 11).
  • Notes and the Log.

⚙︎ → Show Explanations adds a short note under each part saying how it works. Turn it off once you know.

The log

The Log at the end of the plan records everything that happened: each message sent and received, with how it came, every confirmation, changes to the meeting points, and networks, phones and the radio coming and going. It is kept in a file on the phone. Share Log File sends it — useful when asking for support.

05“I'm OK” and “Help”

Two buttons, side by side, hard to mix up: I'm OK in green and Help in red. Tap one and it stays lit.

Your news goes out at once on every network that works — the internet, phones nearby and your Meshtastic radio — sealed so only your family can read it, with where you are. MeetPoint keeps sending it until everyone in the family has confirmed it, and then stops using the other networks. Each family phone confirms on its own; you don't wait for anyone to tap anything.

The lit button rides along with your position: every time it goes out, your family sees again how you are.

Is it through?

Watch your own row at the top of the Family list:

  • ✓Yellow — sent, nobody in the family has it yet.
  • ✓Green (or ⚠red for Help) — someone in the family got it.
  • Back to yellow — the family no longer hears your updates (nobody online, no SMS confirmed lately).

The Log has the details: which networks it went out on, and who confirmed it, how.

Turning it off

Tap the lit button again. The family's phones drop it, and your position goes on alone. If the message also went by text message in the last day, the cancel goes by text message too, so a family member reached only by SMS hears about it.

Arriving at a meeting point

You don't need to tell anyone you arrived. When you get to a meeting point (or an emergency point), MeetPoint tells the family on its own, and their list shows the point's number next to you. When you walk well away from it (about 150 m), it tells them you left.

Your position

MeetPoint sends where you are about every 15 minutes, when you moved, over the internet, phones nearby and the radio. It never goes by text message on its own. Between positions, a small “I'm here” signal tells the family you are still reachable.

07The map

The map shows the meeting points (yellow, numbered), emergency points (red, with a warning sign), the family's dots at their last known place, and you.

People at a meeting point sit as small dots on the edge of its circle. People together somewhere else show as their dots side by side. A red ring around someone's dot means they need help, a green one that they're OK.

Distances

Under the full map, a table shows You and each family member against points 1 to 3: the distance from each one to each point, with the nearest in bold, and how long ago their position came. Tap the coordinates under a name to copy them — to read out to rescuers, or paste in another map app. They turn grey when contact with that person is lost.

Offline map

The streets around each meeting point and the area between them are saved on the phone on their own while there's internet, and again when a point moves. Offline Maps… shows what is saved and how much space it takes. Without internet and without a saved map, MeetPoint shows its Simple Map: the points and the family with distances, but no streets. You can switch to it any time with the button at the top.

08Networks

MeetPoint uses four ways to reach the family, all at once. The icons at the top of the plan show how each is doing right now.

ColourMeaning
GreenWorking now.
YellowSwitched on, but not through yet: Wi-Fi with no answer from the relay, Bluetooth on with no MeetPoint phone in range, a radio connected but no family radio heard yet.
GreyOff: no Wi-Fi or mobile data, Bluetooth off, no radio linked or the radio still connecting.
Struck throughYou switched this network off in Networks (see below).
Blue (iPhone)Nearby with Bluetooth off but Wi-Fi on: other iPhones can still be reached over Wi-Fi, Android phones can't.

1 · Text message (SMS)

Needs only a mobile signal, not internet, and often gets through when calls don't. Sent when you choose to (chapter 09).

2 · Internet

Over Wi-Fi or mobile data, sealed messages go through a relay server. The server can't read them or tell who's talking. It keeps them for 12 hours, so a phone that was offline catches up when it comes back. Everyone in the family must use the same server: ⚙︎ → Internet Relay shows it, whether it's working, and lets you turn the internet off for MeetPoint or use your own ntfy server.

3 · Phones nearby

With no network at all, phones with MeetPoint pass messages to each other over Bluetooth, about 10 to 50 metres apart — iPhone and Android alike. Two iPhones also meet over Wi-Fi.

Each phone keeps the family's recent messages and passes them on to any family phone it meets, also phones of other families, which carry them sealed without being able to read them. “I'm OK”, “Help”, arrivals and cancels are carried for up to 7 days, positions for a day. So a family member who walks from one place to another can carry everyone's news with them.

4 · Meshtastic radio

A small LoRa radio that carries short messages for kilometres, from radio to radio, with no phone network (chapter 10).

Tap the network icons to see each network in words, and how many messages this phone is carrying to pass on.

Switching a network off

All four are on, and that's how they should stay. To test one network on its own, or to save battery, press and hold a network in Networks and confirm with ✓. It shows “Turned off”, and its icon on the plan is struck through. Press and hold it again to switch it back on.

09Text messages

MeetPoint never sends a text message on its own. When you want one to go out, tap Send My Situation by SMS.

The text goes to everyone in the family with a number. It says who you are, how you are (“I'm OK”, “I NEED HELP”, or where you are) and any emergency points, with a MeetPoint link that carries the same news sealed. For example:

Miguel: I'm OK. Open in MeetPoint: https://meetpoint-family.github.io/m/#…

Your messages app opens with everyone's numbers and the text filled in: tap send. Receiving one, tap its MeetPoint link (https://meetpoint-family.github.io/m/#…) to open the news in MeetPoint. What the link carries comes after the #: it never reaches the website, and only your family's MeetPoint can read it.

A family member who can only be reached by SMS gets a confirmation back by SMS: the messages app opens with it ready, one tap to send.

On an iPhone, Messages sends it as an SMS over the mobile network when there's no internet if Settings → Apps → Messages → Send as Text Message is on. With no signal at all, texts wait and go out by themselves when the network comes back.

10Meshtastic radio

A Meshtastic radio is a small LoRa radio that sends short messages over kilometres, from radio to radio, with no phone network at all. If you have one, MeetPoint uses it.

Link your radio

  1. Switch the radio on and keep it close. Close the Meshtastic app first: a radio talks to one app at a time.
  2. In MeetPoint, open ⚙︎ → Meshtastic Radio → Search for Radios.
  3. Tap your radio. If the phone asks for a PIN, it's on the radio's screen (or 123456 on radios without one).

From then on MeetPoint reconnects by itself whenever the radio is on and near. The first connection can take half a minute: the radio tells MeetPoint its settings and every radio it knows.

The radio icon

ColourMeaning
GreyNo radio linked, Bluetooth off, the radio still connecting, or a radio with no region yet.
YellowConnected and set up, but no family member's radio heard yet.
GreenA family member's radio was heard lately, with the time. MeetPoint learns which radios are the family's from their messages.

A radio that's switched off sends nothing, so the icon can only notice it went quiet: yellow again about 45 minutes after it was last heard. While your “I'm OK” or “Help” is on, MeetPoint asks the family radio once every 5 minutes whether it's there — one tiny message, and none when it was heard anyway — so the icon follows within about 12 minutes.

Set Up Radio

A new radio sends nothing until it has a region. Set Up Radio sets its name, region (the law sets which frequencies radios may use where you are) and mode. Tap ✓: the radio restarts to take them, and the radio page shows it saving and then “Set up for MeetPoint”. Radios only hear each other in the same mode: keep Long Fast unless everyone changes. Only these settings change; the radio keeps its channels and still passes on messages for other Meshtastic radios.

What goes over the radio

  • Your “I'm OK”, “Help”, arrivals and position, sealed: other MeetPoint phones on the radio network read them, and nobody else can.
  • Messages from family phones nearby: your phone passes them on to the radio, so they go much further than Bluetooth.
  • With Also Send as Plain Text on (off unless you turn it on), a short readable copy, for example MeetPoint · Miguel: I NEED HELP. 38.71394, -9.13968. Anyone on the channel can read it in the Meshtastic app — handy for rescuers.

Channel picks the radio channel to send on; 0 is the primary channel. Everyone in the family should use the same one.

A radio that was reset

After new firmware or a reset, a radio forgets the phones it knew. On an iPhone, MeetPoint then says Pairing Lost: open Settings → Bluetooth, tap ⓘ next to the radio (it may show its old name), choose Forget This Device, and link it again. Some radios with a touch screen, like the T-Deck, forget phones every time they restart: set their Bluetooth to No PIN (in the radio's own settings, or the Meshtastic app) and they connect without pairing. After a restart, put a touch-screen radio back in Bluetooth mode.

Radio is slow. One message takes a second or two of air time. It's made for short news, which is exactly what MeetPoint sends. A radio left switched on high up — a window, a roof — passes messages on for everyone around.

11Emergency numbers

The Emergency section has a call button for the emergency number of the country you are in: 112 in Portugal and most of Europe, 911 in the US, Canada and Mexico, 999 in the UK, 000 in Australia. Where police, ambulance and fire have different numbers, there's a button for each.

MeetPoint knows the country from where you are, even without internet, and changes it on its own when you cross a border. Tap Country to choose one yourself while your location isn't known. For a country that isn't listed, 112 works from most mobile phones.

My position shows your coordinates and how exact they are. Read them out to rescuers if you can't say where you are.

12A good plan

  • Walk to each meeting point once, together, the way you would go on the day, and place it with Use Where I Am Now.
  • Choose open ground, away from buildings, trees, walls and power lines, that every family member, children included, can find on their own.
  • Children: make sure they know the points without a phone. Write the plan on paper for their school bag.
  • Invite someone who lives far away. When local lines are busy, a call or text to another town often gets through.
  • Test it: turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data on two phones and send “I'm OK” — it should arrive over Bluetooth.
  • Battery: a charged power bank in the emergency bag. Lower the screen brightness on the day.
  • Check it twice a year, for example when the clocks change: new numbers, a new school, a new job.

For the full picture, see your national civil protection authority. In Portugal that's ANEPC (prociv.gov.pt).
